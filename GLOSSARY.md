# Glossary

Shared vocabulary for this project, for humans and agents alike. Use these names in code, plans and conversation. Add terms as they come up, keep entries short, and sort each section alphabetically.

## Editor UI

- **Alt-drag (copy)**: holding `Alt` on a move drag (the body, the x/z arrows, the y arrow) copies instead of moving: the originals stay, and the copies follow the cursor with the selection highlight and a `· copy` label. `Alt` counts at release, so it can be pressed or released mid-drag. On the y arrow a copy snaps onto the originals' tops (a second floor). The copies become the selection. Scale handles and drawing keep `Alt` = from the center (from 05).
- **Array (button) and the array section**: an instance's inspector has **Array** (from 10): the instance becomes an **array** of its entity, one step (`Array instance_3`), with its first item where the instance was, on a path along the instance's local +x (4 items, 1.5 × the entity's width apart). The array's inspector section has the entities it repeats (from 10.2 a list: each row an entity, its weight and its share of the items, + entity adds one), the layout (path · circle · grid · scatter, in a circle or an area) and its fields (a path's place, spacing or count and loop; a circle's or grid's center, radius, count, start and sweep, or columns, rows, layers, spacing, turn and brick stagger), the facing and the item turn, the noise (jitter, turn jitter, the seed and Reroll), the item count (and how many fit), and Edit entity and Detach. Switching layout starts the new one around the first item. A selected array shows its layout as a thin dashed guide, and double-clicking a path array (or **Edit points**) edits its path's points like a line's.
- **convert to free-form**: the inspector's button (Actions) when the selection has boxes or cylinders: each becomes a free-form with the same outline, in one step (`Convert box_3 to freeform_4`). The free-form gets a new ID and takes its original's place, keeping its name, group, kind, color, y and height. A smooth cylinder becomes 4 smooth points (still a true circle or oval), and the free-forms become the selection. The agent converts with `update_nodes { id, type: "freeform" }` (from 06).
- **copy, cut and paste**: `⌘C` / `⌘X` / `⌘V` on the selection, through the browser's copy, cut and paste events and the system clipboard, so they work across scenes, tabs and reloads. The clipboard holds `{ "orlablocks": "nodes", "nodes": [...] }` (the selection with whole subtrees), and anything else is ignored. Paste gives fresh IDs, keeps names and nesting, lands centered in the view (offset snapped to 0.5 m, each box keeping its y), goes into the entered group if there is one, and selects what it pasted. Cut is one step (`Cut box_3`). Off while typing in a field or in the picker (from 05).
- **block style**: the look the editor shares with the Control Panel (from 12): what floats over the view (bars, panels, modals, banners) is a **frame**, a paper card with a 2px ink edge and a hard block shadow (no blur); the rows inside a frame stay calm, with thin dividers. Buttons that act (the active tool, the inspector's labeled buttons, the modals' and Walk menu's) are raised blocks that sink onto their shadow while pressed, with a pastel fill for what's active (sky) or primary (mint). Fields are wells (`--field`) whose edge turns sky on focus. The colors and fonts are the **theme**; the 3D view isn't part of it.
- **box tool**: `B`, drags boxes on the ground with the kind and color from the contextual bar. `Shift` makes the footprint square, `Alt` draws from the center, and `Cmd/Ctrl` turns off snap. Drawing never changes the selection, and the tool stays Box. Replaces the separate room and volume tools (from 03). From 07B it draws on the **surface** under the press (see **drawing on surfaces**).
- **camera**: the 3D perspective camera with a narrow (30°) field of view, so perspective stays mild (from 02). Its pitch is fixed, it orbits the focus point with yaw only, and zoom moves it toward the focus point.
- **canvas**: the full-window 3D drawing surface (the viewport). It fills the whole browser window.
- **capture (clean capture)**: the scene drawn to an image for a **shot** (and, from 09.3, the agent's renders): only the shapes, lit as in the view, without the grid, origin axes, gizmo, selection or hover, hole ghosts or drafts, and without hidden nodes (isolation is ignored). Notes and lines are optional. Each capture renders into a canvas of its own that's never in the page (a react-three-fiber root sized and drawn by hand, `capture.tsx`), so the view never flickers or moves, it can be any size, it works in a tab in the background, and it waits for holes to be cut first (from 09).
- **compass rose**: the small round compass at the bottom right of the view. Its red needle and N point where north (-z) is on screen, turning with the camera's yaw. Clicking it turns the view north-up (yaw 0) (from 06).
- **contextual bar**: the bar that floats just above the tool-bar, with only the kind toggle (room, volume, hole) and the color swatches: for the drawing tools' next shape, or in the Select tool for the selection (kind for a single closed shape, color for all selected shapes). Every other field moved to the **inspector** in 07B (before, it also held sides, walls, taper and bevel, tilt, line and ramp controls, the mirror buttons and the read-only numbers).
- **cylinder tool**: `C`, drags a cylinder's footprint exactly like the box tool (`Shift` makes it a circle, `Alt` from the center, `Cmd/Ctrl` no snap), with the kind and color from the contextual bar and the **sides** from the inspector (from 06).
- **draft**: the live preview of a box while you drag its footprint, drawn lighter, with a `W × D m` label next to the cursor. `Esc` cancels it.
- **drawing on surfaces**: the Box, Cylinder and Pen tools start a shape on the flat surface under the press (from 07B): a volume's top, a room's floor or the top of its walls, whichever is nearest along the view, else the ground. Only level faces count, so slopes (a taper, a bevel), tilted shapes, ramps and sides are passed through, and holes and lines never count. The rest of the drag stays on that level, the new shape's `y` is its height, and a label next to the cursor says where it lands (`on hall's floor · y 3`). The Line and Ramp tools always placed each point on the surface under the cursor (`surfaceUnder` in `pick.ts`).
- **edit points (point editing)**: the mode for reshaping a free-form. Enter it by double-clicking the free-form or with **Edit points** in the inspector. Points show as squares on its top face, and a selected point's handles as dots on stems. Drag points (`Shift`-click selects several, 0.5 m snap, `Cmd/Ctrl` free) or handles (the opposite one stays in line, `Alt` breaks it). Click an edge to add a point without changing the shape, double-click a point for corner ↔ smooth, and `Delete` removes points (at least 3 stay). Each edit is one step (`Reshape freeform_1`), and one that would cross itself shows red and isn't kept. `Esc`, **Done** or clicking outside leaves it (from 06).
- **entity picker**: the button that picks an entity in the inspector (from 10.2: an instance's swap, each row of an array's Entities list): it shows the entity's name and opens a list floating over everything, with the Entities panel's fuzzy search (name, #tag, @skill), each entity's tags and size, ↑ / ↓ and Enter, Esc or a click outside to close (`EntityPicker.tsx`).
- **Entities panel**: the floating panel with the project's entities (from 08.4; under the outliner until it floated like the inspector), each with how often it's placed. Dragged by its header (double-click puts it back, right of the outliner), collapsible, remembered per viewer, and scrolling past a max height. Its **search** is fuzzy (letters in order, `wdldr` finds `Wooden ladder`) over each entity's name, tags and skills (named in its description, or acting on its tags); `#word` searches only tags, `@word` only skills, and every word must match (`search.ts`). Drag a row into the view, or press its place button and click, to place one; click a row to edit its name, description and tags, or delete it.
- **Edit entity (mode)**: editing an entity's shapes (from 08.5): double-click an instance, or its inspector's **Edit entity**, or the Entities panel's edit button. The store then holds the entity's definition, with its own undo history (`entities/<id>/history.jsonl`) and camera, a blue banner says what's being edited and where it's placed, the project bar ends in its name, and the origin is its pivot. Every step saves it and redraws every instance, in every tab. Notes, instances, placing and Make entity are off; a top-level hole is fine (every instance is a group). **Back** (or `Esc` with nothing selected) reopens the scene, whose history is untouched. The agent sees `editing` in `get_scene`.
- **entered group**: the group you've double-clicked into. Clicks, the marquee and `Cmd/Ctrl+A` then act on its children, and the inspector's header says `lobby › …`. `Esc` or clicking outside it leaves it (from 03). From 07B, what you draw there (Box, Cylinder, Pen, Line, Ramp) goes into it, like a paste.
- **floor-follow**: while walking, the eye stays at eye height above the floor under the feet: one ray straight down from the feet plus a **step height** (0.45 m) against the rendered (cut) meshes, standing on the highest face that faces up, else the ground. Stairs and ramps are climbed, taller things are walked through at the floor's height (there's no collision), and a floor hole drops you. The eye eases up quickly and down a bit slower. `E` / `Q` float free of it, `F` lands (`floorUnder` in `walk.ts`, from 09).
- **error panel**: the red `N errors` pill at the top right, hidden while there are none. It opens a list of what went wrong in this tab: a render crash (the 3D view shows *Reload the view* instead of going blank), an uncaught error or rejection, an error from the server. Click an entry to see its stack. **Copy** copies the log and **Clear** empties it (from 06).
- **focus point**: the ground point under the center of the screen. The camera orbits it and pans move it (from 02).
- **frame guide**: while walking, the aspect ratio shots are cropped to (16:9 by default; 4:3, 1:1, 21:9 or none, per viewer), shown as a frame with the rest dimmed. The **player camera**'s field of view is horizontal across it, as in a game, so a wider window sees more around it (from 09).
- **graybox**: the look of the view: warm near-white matte materials with a 1 m tile texture, a sun with soft shadows and faint outlines, like a blocked-out game level.
- **grid**: the reference lines on the ground plane: a line every 1 m and a stronger one every 5 m, fading out away from the focus point. It's a measuring and snapping aid only and is never part of the scene data. The view bar's **grid** toggle hides it (from 07B).
- **hand tool**: `H`, navigation only: drag to pan and scroll to zoom toward the focus point. Holding `Space` switches to it temporarily from any tool (from 03; in 02 it also selected).
- **height handle**: the small yellow cube on the top center of a single selected box, part of the transform gizmo. Dragging it changes the box's height with the bottom fixed, in 0.05 m steps (`Cmd/Ctrl` for free), with a live `h … m` label. It replaces 02's height gizmo (the blue stem and cone) (from 03).
- **info-label**: the status bar docked along the bottom edge, full width, one line: the connection state, the cursor's ground position (x · z), the camera yaw, the room, volume and hole counts (the **Show holes** toggle was here in 07, and moved to the **view bar** in 07B), and the current tool's hint on the right (a short notice, in yellow, can replace the hint for a moment). The numbers are fixed-width so it never jitters. The 3D view ends above it (a small top-right box before 03, a floating row across the top from 03, the bottom bar from 05, when the project bar moved out of it).
- **isolate**: showing only one node and what's in it (from 07B), with the outliner row's isolate button or `I` (on the single selected node; `I` again, the view bar's ✕, or `Esc` with nothing selected shows everything). Isolating a group enters it, so what's drawn next goes in it. Anything new while isolated (drawn, pasted, the agent's) shows too. Hidden nodes can't be seen, picked, drawn on or snapped to, but holes outside the isolation still cut what shows. For this tab only, not an edit; the agent sees it as `view.isolated`.
- **inspector**: the floating panel with the fields of the selection, or of the drawing tool's next shape (from 07B). Its header names what it shows (`lobby (box_3)`, `3 selected`, `next box`, `lobby › box_3` in an entered group); it's dragged by the header (double-click puts it back top right), collapses to it, remembers both per viewer and stays on screen. Below the header: the read-only numbers, then sections: Shape (sides), Walls (thickness), Profile (taper, bevel), Tilt (pitch, roll, Reset tilt), Ramp (width, smooth or steps, base, Reverse), Line (thickness, dashes, arrows, Reverse) and Actions (mirror X / Z, Convert to free-form, Edit points). Sliders preview on the shapes while dragged and send one step on release; fields send on `Enter` or when left (`Inspector.tsx`). From 10.2 its choice buttons (an array's layout, a ramp's base, a line's dashes and arrows) are icons, with their words as tooltips.
- **Library panel**: the panel docked on the right (from 08), toggled by the book button next to the project bar: the project's **Skills**, **Tags** and **Guide** in tabs. Rows show the name, the description's first line and the uses; clicking one edits it in place (name, which renames; description; a skill's tags; Delete). It has its own Undo / Redo (and `⌘Z` / `⇧⌘Z` while focus is in it outside a text field), separate from the scene's. The guide saves a second after typing stops. With it open, the inspector's default place moves left of it.
- **lock (`locked`)**: a node marked `locked: true` (from 07B, with the outliner row's lock button or `update_nodes`): it and everything in it can't be clicked, hovered, marquee-selected or picked by `Cmd/Ctrl+A` in the view, but still count as surfaces to draw on and to snap to (draw on an island without grabbing it). The outliner still selects a locked node, and then it edits normally. Locking and unlocking are undo steps (`Lock box_3`). A human's aid: the agent may still change locked nodes, and its instructions ask it to leave them alone unless asked.
- **line tool**: `L`, draws a line: each click places a point on the surface under the cursor (a volume's top, a room's floor or wall top, else the ground; x/z snap to 0.5 m, `Cmd/Ctrl` no snap), click-drag pulls out handles for a curve, and a double-click or `Enter` finishes. `Backspace` removes the last point and `Esc` drops the line. The preview shows it in the contextual bar's style (color, black by default, thickness, dashes, arrows) (from 06).
- **marquee**: the rectangle you drag on empty ground with the Select tool to select every box it touches on screen (its exact outline, not its bounds), live as you drag. `Shift` adds to the selection and `Esc` restores the selection it started from (from 03).
- **origin axes**: a marker at the world origin, with +x as a red bar, +y as a green bar and +z as a blue bar (the gizmo's colors), for orientation while rotating.
- **Make entity**: the inspector's action (from 08.4) when the selection has shapes and no instances or notes: it asks for a name, then the selection becomes a new **entity** and one **instance** takes its place, selected (`Make entity poison`, one scene step; undo puts the shapes back, and the entity stays in the library). A single group is unwrapped: its contents are the entity, and its name, description and tags the entity's. The agent's `make_entity`.
- **Note tool**: `N`, pins a **note**: a click puts it on the surface under the cursor (a top, a floor, else the ground), into the entered group, selects it and puts the caret in its text in the inspector; the tool goes back to Select. The contextual bar picks the next note's color (from 08).
- **outliner**: the collapsible tree panel on the left listing every node by name (an unnamed one by its ID; no separate ID column, the inspector's header shows it) with a room / volume / group icon (dashed for a room, solid for a volume; a square for a box, a circle for a cylinder, a squircle for a free-form, from 06). Click selects (`Shift` or `Cmd/Ctrl` toggles), double-click renames (double-clicking the icon **frames** the node instead: the camera flies to fit it, keeping its yaw), dragging a row drops it before, after or into a group, and hovering a row highlights it in the view (from 03). From 07B each row has an **eye** (hide), a **lock** and an **isolate** button (on hover, or while on), and rows that don't show in the view are dimmed.
- **pause menu**: what `Esc` shows while walking (the browser releases the pointer lock, which pauses): the **player camera** (preset, eye height, field of view, speed, and for third person the boom and the human), this walk's options (floor-follow, crosshair, **frame guide**, shot size, notes and lines in shots), the shots taken during the walk, and **Continue** (`Enter`, relocking the pointer; if Chrome refuses right after an Esc, a click continues) or **Exit** (`Esc` again), which flies back to the editor camera from before the walk (from 09).
- **profile knobs**: small spheres on a single selected closed shape, part of the transform gizmo (from 07B): a room's **wall knob** (brick) on its wall's inner face halfway up, and a volume's or hole's **taper knob** (teal) on its top and **bevel knob** (pink) on its side where the rounding starts, on opposite sides between an edge's midpoint and a corner (clear of the scale handles). Dragging the taper knob toward the center tapers, the bevel knob down the side rounds (no bevel knob once the top is a point), the wall knob across the wall thickens (up to what leaves an inside). They snap to 0.05 (`Cmd/Ctrl` for free), preview live with a `taper 0.40` label, and each drag is one step. They work on tilted shapes, in the shape's own frame.
- **placing**: putting an instance in the view (from 08.4): drag an entity from the **Entities panel** onto the view, or press its place button and click. It lands on the surface under the cursor, in the entered group, and is selected; `Esc` stops placing.
- **pen tool**: `P`, draws a free-form: click for a corner point, click and drag for a smooth one (the drag pulls out its handles, not snapped), click the first point or press `Enter` to close, `Backspace` removes the last point, `Esc` drops the outline. Points snap to 0.5 m (`Cmd/Ctrl` no snap). The preview turns red when the path crosses itself, and a closing outline that would is refused with a notice. The outline survives Space-panning (from 06). From 07B its first point sets the surface the free-form stands on (see **drawing on surfaces**).
- **project bar**: `Castle ▸ Entrance`, the button at the top left, above the outliner, as wide as the names need (at least the outliner's width, at most 640 px): the open project and scene. Clicking it opens the project picker (from 04; its own button from 05, first in the info-label before).
- **project picker**: the modal for projects and scenes: projects on the left (double-click renames, **New project** shows the create project form), and the selected project's description and scenes on the right. Clicking a scene opens it for every tab and the agent. Each scene row has rename and duplicate buttons on hover, and a duplicate copies the history and opens the copy. With nothing open it can't be dismissed, and with no projects at all it's only the **create project** form (name, description, first scene) (from 04).
- **repeat copy (`Alt+J`)**: copies the current selection by the last Alt-drag copy's offset and selects the copies, so pressing it again continues a row. The offset is remembered per tab and forgotten when the scene changes. With nothing to repeat, the info-label says "Alt-drag a copy first" (from 05).
- **ramp**: a shape that's a path with a width, walked along its top: a ramp, stairs, a landing, a walkway or a spiral stair (`type: "ramp"`, ID `ramp_N`). Its `points` are the centerline in absolute world x/z, each with the surface's height `y` there, and flat handles `in` / `out` curve it. Between two points the height changes evenly with the distance along the ground, so two points at the same y make a landing. `step` makes it stairs: each edge gets round(rise / step) equal steps, the top one flush with the higher end. `base` is `solid` (filled down to just below its lowest point) or `floating` (a 0.2 m slab under its surface). Always a volume (never a room, and never a hole since 07B), with no x, z, y, height or rotation: moving, turning and mirroring change its points. A turn too tight for its width is refused. The agent can give a `spiral` instead of points. Drawn with the **ramp tool** (from 07).
- **ramp tool**: `R`, draws a ramp like the Line tool draws a line: each click places a point on the surface under the cursor (so the floor, then a platform's top, is a ramp in two clicks), click-drag curves it, and a double-click or `Enter` finishes. The inspector sets the width, smooth or the step height, and solid or floating, the contextual bar the color, and the preview is a draft of the ramp. Selected ramps get the same controls plus **Reverse**. Double-click a ramp to edit its points, as a line's (from 07).
- **rotate handle**: the purple, nearly closed ring just outside the (-x, -z) top corner of the **selection frame**. Dragging it rotates the selection around the frame's center, with 15° snaps of the frame's angle (`Cmd/Ctrl` for free) and a live angle label (from 03; turning around the selection frame from 06).
- **scale handles**: the 8 small white squares on the corners and edge midpoints of a single selected box's top face, turned with the box. Dragging one resizes the box in its own axes with the opposite side fixed: `Shift` keeps the aspect ratio, `Alt` scales from the center, `Cmd/Ctrl` turns off snap (from 03) A tilted shape's sit on its own tilted top face and keep the opposite side fixed in the world, and its height handle sits on that face's center and keeps its bottom fixed (from 07B).
- **selection frame**: the rectangle the gizmo sits on. For a single box or cylinder it's the shape's own footprint rectangle. For anything else it's the frame around the selection, which turns as you rotate it and stays turned while the same selection stays selected (Figma-style). Its center is the rotate pivot, so rotating back lands where you started. An undo or someone else's change resets it to axis-aligned (`selectionFrame` in `geometry.ts`) (from 06).
- **select tool**: `V`, click to select (`Shift`-click adds or removes), drag a box to move the selection, drag empty ground for a marquee, `Cmd/Ctrl+A` selects all. It pans only with `Space` or the middle button (from 03).
- **selection**: the nodes currently selected in the editor, highlighted with a blue tint and outline (a group highlights all its boxes; hovering what a click would select tints it yellow) and described in the inspector. The Select tool selects on click (the outermost group, or inside an **entered group** its child), `Esc` deselects, `Delete` removes the selection, and drawing never changes it (from 03). It's also reported to the server as **selection (scene)**.
- **sides control**: in the inspector for the Cylinder tool and a selected cylinder: a smooth toggle (a circle icon), then − / the count / +. From smooth, − or + switch to the last count used (8 at first). Typing a count and pressing Enter sets it, clamped to 3..64 (from 06).
- **tool-bar**: the floating bar at the bottom center, with a margin from the window edges and the info-label. It holds only controls: the big icon tool buttons (Select, Hand, Box, with the shortcut letter in the corner), then Undo, Redo and Clear icon buttons. Counts and hints live in the info-label (from 03).
- **stats readout**: the view bar's `stats` toggle (from 10.5, remembered per viewer): a small readout at the top of the view with the CPU time of a frame (a rolling average over 30), its draw calls (shadows included), its triangles, and the shapes drawn (instances and arrays expanded). The view draws on demand, so it changes while something moves.
- **Show notes**: the view bar's `notes` toggle (from 08): off, notes aren't drawn and can't be clicked, unless selected. For this tab only.
- **Shots panel**: the floating panel with the open document's **shots** as thumbnails, newest first (from 09). Bottom right by default, dragged by its header, collapsible, and opened from the view bar's shots button (or by the first shot). Click a thumbnail to fly back to its view. Each has a caption (double-click), Copy image, Save and Delete (two clicks: shots aren't undoable), and an agent badge or an amber dot when the level changed since. **Save all** downloads one zip (`ShotsPanel.tsx`).
- **shutter**: `K`, or the camera button on the **view bar**: a shot of the view exactly as framed, at its size on screen in device pixels (at most 4096 px on the long edge), with a short white flash (from 09).
- **tags field**: the inspector's chips for a single group or shape's tags (not a line's): the field completes from the library, `Enter` adds, Backspace in the empty field drops the last chip, and a new name offers **Create #name** (it adds the tag to the library, then to the node) (from 08).
- **theme and the theme toggle**: the UI's colors and fonts (from 12), shared by the editor and the Control Panel (`src/ui/theme.css`): **light** (minty paper and ink) or **dark** (the ink variant), in Fredoka (titles), Nunito (text) and JetBrains Mono (numbers), bundled with the app. The view bar's last button, the **theme toggle**, cycles **System** (the default: follow the OS) → **Light** → **Dark**, remembered per browser. The 3D view stays light in both.
- **tilt (`pitch`, `roll`)**: a box or cylinder volume's turn around its own x axis (`pitch`, + leans its top toward its local +z) and its own z axis (`roll`, + toward its local -x), in degrees through its center, roll first, then pitch, then `rotation` (so turning it never changes its tilt). Stored in -180..180 and left out at 0. `y` stays the bottom before tilting, and `height` the length along the tilted axis: a cylinder lying on its side is pitch 90. `get_scene` gives a tilted shape its real `bounds`. Mirroring flips the pitch (on Z) or the roll (on X). Free-forms and rooms don't tilt, and a tilted shape can't convert. Set with the **tilt rings** or the inspector's pitch and roll fields, and **Reset tilt** levels it (from 07). From 14 every closed shape tilts (rooms and free-forms too: a free-form around the world's x and z axes through its outline's centroid), and so do instances (around their pivot) and arrays (every item, around the layout's anchor). **Tilting as one** (`rotate_nodes` with `axis: "x"` or `"z"`, `transform_nodes` `tilt`) turns several nodes rigidly about one pivot: each place orbits it and each tilt composes.
- **tilt rings**: two rings of the transform gizmo on a single box or cylinder volume, around its center: red for pitch (square to its own x axis, which stays level) and blue for roll (square to its own z axis, as tilted by the pitch). Dragging one turns the shape around that axis, snapping to 15° (`Cmd/Ctrl` for free), with a `pitch 30°` label. While a shape is tilted, its scale and height handles hide (from 07). From 14 any selection has them (a group, several nodes, a free-form, an instance): around the world's x and z axes through the middle of its bounds, tilting it all as one (one `rotate_nodes`). The inspector's Tilt section has pitch and roll for every closed shape, instance and array, and a **yaw** row for boxes, cylinders and instances.
- **transform gizmo**: the handles on the selection, in the Select tool only: body drag and x/z arrows to move, a y arrow to raise, 8 footprint handles and a height handle to scale, a rotate handle (from 03). Replaces the height gizmo.
- **Walk tool / walk mode**: `W` (or its button dragged onto the view): drops a camera onto the surface clicked, flies down to the eye, and locks the pointer. The mouse looks, `WASD` walks along the ground, `E` / `Q` float, `F` lands, `Shift` is 3 × as fast, the wheel sets the speed, a click (or `K`) takes a **shot** cropped to the **frame guide**, and `Esc` pauses (the **pause menu**). **First person** or **third person** (the **preset**, in the contextual bar): the same walk, with the camera at the eye or over the shoulder of the `human` entity (the **avatar**; a capsule without one). While walking the panels hide, the view shows no grid, gizmo, highlights or hole ghosts, and the agent sees `view.walking`. A camera, not a game: no physics or collision (`walk.ts`, `WalkScreens.tsx`, from 09).
- **welcome**: what the editor shows with no scene open (from 12): the paper and its grid instead of the view, the large **wordmark** rising in with the tagline, and the project picker's card below it; before the server connects, "Connecting to the server…" in its place.
- **wordmark**: the title **orla BLOCKS** (from 12): "Orla", then six pastel letter blocks (`src/ui/Wordmark.tsx`). Large on the Control Panel, the welcome and above the project picker (rising in only where it's an arrival: the welcome and the Control Panel); small and still at the editor's top left, before the project bar. The product's name in text is **OrlaBlocks**; `orlablocks` stays the identifier (package, MCP server, skill).
- **yaw**: rotation of the camera around the vertical axis through the focus point. It's the only rotation the camera has (A/D or ←/→).
- **3D cursor**: a small sphere and ring where the pointer meets the scene (a shape's surface, lying on its face, or the ground), a constant size on screen, with its x, y, z in the status bar (from 14). The view bar's **cursor** toggle. The agent sees where it last rested as `view.pointer` (with the node's `id`), so "here" means something.
- **released (walk)**: a paused walk with the menu closed and the mouse free (from 14): the pause menu's **Release mouse** (or Tab), or a refused pointer lock. A chip says so; clicks in the view don't take the mouse back (so clicking to focus the window is safe), and Enter or the chip's **Continue** does. For working in a second window while this one shows the walk.
- **uniform scale handle**: the gizmo's gold cube just outside the selection's top corner opposite the rotate handle (from 14), on any selection with something that has a size (groups and several nodes too): drag it out or in to scale everything about the bottom center of its bounds, 0.05 steps (Shift 0.25, Cmd/Ctrl free), the factor in the label. One step (`transform_nodes`). The single shape's scale handles still stretch it along its own axes. The inspector's **Scale…** (Actions) does the same by a typed factor, and instances and arrays have a **scale** row.
- **Work with agent**: the top bar's robot button (from 14): the open scene becomes the **agent's scene**, and the agent keeps working there while the human opens other scenes of the same project (a design race). A chip then names it (**agent here**, or **agent: <scene>**, a click opens it) with ✕ to stop; the project picker badges it. Opening another project ends it.

## Scene and data

- **actor**: who made a change, either `human` (the editor) or `agent` (MCP). Stored on each box as `createdBy`. Not shown in the view since the graybox look.
- **array**: a node that repeats entities on a **layout**, live (`array_N`, from 10): `entities` (one or more, each with an optional `weight`), `layout`, `facing?`, `rotation?` (added to every item's facing), `jitter?` and `turnJitter?` (seeded noise, meters and ± degrees), `seed?` and `skip?` (item indices left out). Its **items** are instances the layout places, at most 500. It adds no level for holes: its items cut and are cut as instances placed where the array is, so a window array in a tower's group cuts the tower's walls. Counted as `1 array (10 items)`. **Detach** turns it into a group of plain instances. `src/shared/arrays.ts` places the items.
- **array edit mode**: an array's editing in the view (from 10.4): double-click it, or **Edit items** in the inspector; Esc or a click outside leaves it. Every item shows a dot at its pivot (a skipped one hollow); click a dot (Shift adds), and ⌫ skips the selected items or brings skipped ones back (one step); **restore all** in the inspector brings every one back. Orange handles drag the layout: a circle's or scatter circle's center (it snaps to shapes' centers within 0.75 m, else the 0.5 m grid) and radius (Shift: a circle's start), and a grid's center and far-corner item, which sets its spacing (Shift: equal). A path's or a scatter area's points edit as a line's or a free-form's (`arrayEdit.ts`).
- **batch ref**: a name given to a `draw_shapes` entry (`ref: "chamber"`), used as `$chamber` by later entries of the same call where an ID goes (`parent`, `layout.along.id`), so a group and what's in it, or a line and the array following it, arrive in one call (from 13). The result maps each ref to its ID.
- **compact result**: what the agent's edit tools return (from 13): per node its id, type, kind, name, parent and bounds, with a count instead of points, an instance's entity and pivot, and an array's layout (points as a count, so a following array never echoes its target), item count and **item lines**. `verbose: true` returns the nodes in full. From 14 an array gives its item count and range of tops, and its item lines only with `items: true`; `get_scene`'s open notes come short (id, label, point, the start of the text; `notes: "full"` for all of it).
- **define entity (`define_entity`)**: the agent's way to make an entity from shapes given around its pivot (the bottom center at the origin), never drawn in the scene: no scene step, no instance to clean up (from 13). `make_entity` stays for shapes already in the scene (`keep: false` leaves nothing in their place).
- **instanced drawing (arrays)**: how the view draws an array's items (from 10.5): each part of each entity once per array, as an `InstancedMesh` with a matrix per item (body and a room's floor), and all the items' edges as one merged set of line segments, cut by the entity's own holes. An item cut by a hole from outside its entity, the entities' holes (ghosts) and a missing entity's items are drawn shape by shape as before (`instancing.ts`, `InstancedArrays.tsx`).
- **array item**: one placed copy in an array (from 10): an instance with a place and a turn from the layout, virtual (`array_3/7`, its shapes `array_3/7/box_2`; `ownerOf` maps them to the array), numbered from 0 in layout order. Drawn, picked (a hit selects the array) and cut like an instance, never edited alone.
- **bevel**: a volume's `bevel`, a fraction from 0 (a sharp top edge, left out of the data) to 1 (as round as it fits): the top edge rounds along a quarter circle of radius bevel × min(height, the top's half smallest extent). A tall smooth cylinder at 1 gets a dome. The bottom stays flat. Set with the inspector's bevel slider, which previews while dragged (from 07).
- **box**: a footprint plus a height: `{ id, type: "box", name?, kind, x, z, y, width, depth, height, rotation, color, createdBy }` in meters. `x, z` is the footprint's **center** (from 03, it was the min corner in 02), `width` and `depth` run along the box's own axes, and it rises from `y` to `y + height`. Its kind is either **room** or **volume** and can change. Its ID is `box_N`, never reused (from 03, it was per kind in 02).
- **command**: one user-level edit and one undo step. It's made of ops, records its actor, and stores its inverse (from 02).
- **compass**: the fixed directions of every scene: north is -z, south +z, east +x, west -x (y up). At yaw 0 the camera looks north. `get_scene` reports it as `compass`, and the server instructions state it, so "the north wall" means the same to the human and the agent (from 06).
- **copy (duplicate)**: a node copied with its whole subtree: new IDs, the same names, structure and parent group, offset by `dx, dy, dz` (`count` copies make a row, copy i offset i times). It goes right after its original in the list, and every copy call is one step (`Copy group_1 ×3`). The store's `duplicateNodes`, the `duplicate_nodes` message, and `move_nodes` with `copy: true` for the agent (from 05).
- **counts**: what a set of nodes holds, as one line: `3 rooms · 2 volumes · 1 hole · 1 group`, leaving out what there's none of (`countsText` in `tree.ts`). The info-label shows the scene's, the inspector a single group's, and the agent's outline gives every group's as `contains` (from 08).
- **cylinder**: a shape with exactly a box's fields plus an optional `sides`. Its footprint is the ellipse inscribed in its width × depth rectangle (an oval when they differ), turned by `rotation`. With `sides` (3..64) it's a regular polygon on that ellipse, with a flat edge facing its local +x (an octagon has flat walls facing the axes). Without, it's smooth (drawn with 64 segments, lit as round). A room or a volume like a box. Mirroring an odd-sided one on X sets `180 − rotation`. ID `cylinder_N` (from 06).
- **data folder**: where the server saves everything: `./data` where it runs (gitignored), or `DATA_DIR`. It holds `app.json` (the last open scene), `projects/`, and a `.lock` so only one server uses it (from 04).
- **description (group)**: a group's free text saying what that part of the level is ("entry hall, safe zone"), for people and the agent. Set in the inspector (a single group, sent on leaving the field or `⌘Enter`), with `update_nodes` or with `group_nodes`; the outliner shows it as the row's tooltip, and the agent's outline shows it on every group (from 08).
- **design guide**: the project's `rules/design-guide.md`, markdown: what a good level is for this game (goals, the game's facts, principles, common failures, a worked example, house rules), modeled on ClaudeAnimationBase's ANIMATION_GUIDE.md. Every project starts with a default (seeded once; a deleted one stays deleted), edited in the Library panel's Guide tab, part of the library's history. The agent reads it with `get_guide design`, and the outline's `guide` line says its size and when it changed (from 08).
- **detach**: turning an instance back into a plain group of world copies of its entity's shapes, with new IDs, where it was, named after the instance or else the entity (`Detach instance_3`, one step). Its shapes no longer follow the entity. The inspector's Detach, the agent's `detach_instances` (from 08.4).
- **editor state**: a scene's `editor.json`: the camera (focus, yaw, zoom distance) and the selection. It's written 500 ms after they stop changing, and right away before switching scenes or stopping the server. It's restored when the scene opens. It isn't design data and isn't undoable (from 04).
- **elevation (`y`)**: the height of a box's bottom above the ground, in meters. 0 = on the ground; negative is below ground (from 03).
- **entity**: a prefab in the project library (from 08.4): an ID (a slug of its first name, never changed), a name, a description, tags, and a **definition**: its shapes and groups around a **pivot** at the origin (its bottom center), in `entities/<id>/entity.json`, laid out like a scene. It can't hold instances or notes. Its record is in `library.json` (edits and deletion are library steps; making one isn't, like creating a scene). A deleted entity keeps its folder, so undo brings it back; one that's placed anywhere can't be deleted. The editor and the server each keep the open project's definitions in a registry (`entities.ts`).
- **footprint**: a closed shape's outline on the ground, as a polygon in world x/z (`footprint()` in `geometry.ts`; a box's is its 4 corners). Its meshes and room walls are built from it, so a new shape type only adds its footprint and its move / rotate / mirror cases (from 06). For a room it's the outside of its walls, which grow inward from it (from 07; the wall centerline before).
- **expanded (instance)**: an instance as the view, picking, the marquee and holes see it: a virtual group with the instance's ID and place, holding its entity's shapes in world space with IDs like `instance_4/box_2` (`expandInstance`, cached per instance). A hit on one selects its instance (`ownerOf`). So for holes an instance is a group (from 08.4).
- **follow (array)**: a path array whose path is another node's (from 10.3): `layout.along: { id, at?, offset? }`. A box, cylinder or free-form gives its outline, a loop counterclockwise seen from above, at its top (a tapered volume's top ring) or bottom, `offset` m inward (default half a room's wall: on the wall's centerline; 0 for a volume); a ramp its centerline at the surface's height and a line its path, `offset` m to the right of travel. The store keeps the array's own `points` up to date in the same step as every change to what it follows, and the view follows a drag live. A following array has no place of its own (moving it alone is refused). **Unlink** (`along: null`) keeps the path it has; removing the followed node unlinks it too (`remove_nodes` says `unlinked`), converting it to a free-form keeps the follow, and a copy follows the copied target or is unlinked. In the inspector, **Follow a shape** then a click in the view (Esc cancels); a followed shape's info says `followed by array_2`.
- **facing (array)**: how an array turns its items (from 10): `along` a path (the default there) or `tangent` to a circle (the default there) turn the entity's local +x along the way; `out` and `in` turn it away from or toward a circle's center; `fixed` keeps it as drawn (a grid's items turn with the grid); `random` turns each anyhow. The array's `rotation` is added to it.
- **free-form**: a shape whose footprint is a closed outline of **points** in absolute world x/z (3 to 500; the last joins the first), each with optional bezier handles `in` / `out` as offsets from it (a point without them is a corner). It has `y`, `height`, `kind` and `color`, but no center, size or rotation: moving, rotating, mirroring and scaling change its points (a mirror keeps their order). Its outline may not cross itself. Room walls are the outline offset ±0.1 m with Clipper2, so concave outlines get clean walls. ID `freeform_N` (from 06).
- **group**: a node that contains other nodes (via their `parent`), with ID `group_N`. It has no position of its own: its bounds come from its boxes (`get_scene` reports them as `bounds`), and moving, rotating or deleting it acts on everything inside as one command. A group left empty disappears in the same command (from 03). From 08 it can have a **description**.
- **handle frame**: a shape's own rectangle: a box's or cylinder's footprint rectangle (turned with it), a free-form's world-axis bounds (`handleFrame` in `geometry.ts`). The gizmo sits on the **selection frame**, which for a single box or cylinder is this (from 06).
- **hole**: the third kind of closed shape (`kind: "hole"`, next to room and volume): any box, cylinder or free-form that cuts itself out of other shapes when drawn (a door, a window, an arch, a hole in a floor). For a hole in group G, whose parent is P (the top level when G is top-level), it cuts the shapes directly in G, directly in P (beside G), and directly in G's and P's sibling groups, so a `door` group next to a room's walls cuts them and the walls of the rooms beside that room (the rule since 07B). It never cuts holes or lines, and only cuts what it overlaps. A hole outside any group cuts nothing, and shows a yellow warning diamond in the view and a warning in the outliner. It cuts a room's floor only if it reaches below the room's `y`. Default height 2.2 m, and it can taper, bevel and tilt. Drawn as a translucent ghost with dashed edges, and picked like a solid shape. The cut happens only when drawing (manifold, in the browser): the data stays plain shapes (`holes.ts`, `csg.ts`) (from 07).
- **hide (`hidden`)**: a node marked `hidden: true` (from 07B, with the outliner row's eye or `update_nodes`): it and everything in it aren't drawn, so they can't be picked, drawn on or snapped to, and a hidden hole cuts nothing. The outliner still lists and selects it (dimmed, with the gizmo on it). Hiding and showing are undo steps (`Hide box_3`, `Show box_3`) and are saved with the scene. A human's aid, like **lock**; the agent sees `hidden` and knows the node still exists.
- **glossary (outline)**: the part of `get_scene` that explains the tags and skills the result shows or names, one level deeper (a skill's tags, what their descriptions name), each with its description, so the agent rarely needs `get_library` (from 08).
- **history**: the single linear list of commands shared by the human and the agent. Undo reverts the latest command, whoever made it (from 02) From 04 it's per scene and survives restarts, rebuilt from the **history log**.
- **history log**: a scene's `history.jsonl`, append-only, one line per step (`commit` with its ops and inverse, `undo`, `redo`), numbered by `seq`. `scene.json` records the `seq` it includes. The log is written first, so after a crash it can be one step ahead, and opening the scene applies that step (from 04).
- **item line**: where an array's item is, as the agent reads it (from 13): `array_5/3 → 12.1, 4.5, -8 · top 5 · 90°` (its pivot, the height of its top and its turn). In compact results (the first 40), in `get_scene { root: "array_5" }` (all) and in `find_nodes { type: "item" }` (with `near`, nearest first).
- **line**: an annotation shape: an open path of 2 or more points in absolute world x/y/z (each point has its own y), with optional 3D bezier handles, a palette `color` (black by default), a `thickness` in screen pixels (1..12, constant at any zoom), `dashed` and `arrow` (none, end or both). It has no kind, elevation or height. It's drawn over the shapes (fainter behind them), casts no shadow, and is picked within a few pixels of its path. Selected, it keeps its color and gets a blue halo with rings on its points (hovered: a yellow halo). Moving, rotating and mirroring change its points, and **Reverse** flips its direction. Point editing works on it, with a y arrow on the selected point. ID `line_N` (from 06).
- **layout (array)**: where an array's items go (from 10): a `path` (a line's points, open or closed, with `place`: every `spacing` m fitted so the items land evenly, `count` evenly, on the `corners`, or at the `midpoints` of the edges), a `circle` (a center, a radius, a count, `start` from 0 = east and `sweep` for an arc), a `grid` (a center, a turn, columns, rows, layers, spacing and a brick `stagger`), or a `scatter` (from 10.2: items at random from the seed in a circle or an `area` outline edited like a free-form, at least `minDistance` apart, fewer when they can't all fit; its `rotation` turns the frame, so turning the array turns the same pattern).
- **mesh (shape mesh)**: a closed shape as triangles, built in `mesh.ts`: `shapeMesh` gives the parts it's drawn with in its own frame (a volume's solid, or a room's walls and floor slab), and `hitMesh` what picking and the marquee test in world space (the body, plus a room's floor at its elevation). Meshes are watertight and share vertices (from 07).
- **mirror**: flipping nodes in place on a world axis, X (east ↔ west) or Z, across the center of their combined footprint bounds. Each box's center reflects and its rotation becomes -rotation, and `y` never changes. A group mirrors as a unit, and mirroring twice restores the exact values. `Shift+X` / `Shift+Z`, the mirror buttons in the inspector's Actions (a flip icon, turned a quarter for Z, with a red X or blue Z in the corner), and the `mirror_nodes` tool. Mirroring only works in place: to mirror a copy, Alt-drag it first (from 05).
- **name**: an optional human-friendly label on any node ("lobby"). Not unique. Tools still take IDs (from 03).
- **node**: anything in the scene's flat list: a box or a group. Each has an `id`, an optional `name` and an optional `parent` (from 03).
- **human (entity)**: the entity every project starts with (from 08.5): a person for scale, 1.8 m tall, standing on its pivot and facing north, made of plain gray shapes to edit to match the player. Seeded once (`library.json`'s `seeded`), so one a project deleted stays deleted.
- **instance**: a placed copy of an entity (`instance_N`, from 08.4): `entity`, `x, y, z` (where the pivot goes), `rotation` (around the pivot), a name, a parent, and nothing else of its own: its shapes, description and tags are its entity's, so editing the entity changes it. It moves, turns and copies like a shape; mirroring moves it and turns it to face the mirrored way without flipping the entity. Swapping its `entity` keeps its place. One whose entity is missing shows as a red block. The outliner shows it by its name or its entity's; `get_scene` gives it one line with its `bounds`, and the glossary its entity.
- **library**: the project's tags and skills, and its design guide, shared by all its scenes (from 08): `library.json` (tags, skills, `seq`, `seeded`), `library-history.jsonl` (its own undo history, apart from every scene's) and `rules/design-guide.md`. Edited as one step per `update_library` call or editor edit (`Add #light`, `Rename #light to #liftable`, `Edit design guide`); ops set or remove a tag or skill by name, or set the guide's text. Tags and skills are sorted by name.
- **alias**: a tag's or skill's old name, kept when it's renamed, so `#light` still means `#liftable` in text, tag lists and `find_nodes`; nothing is rewritten. A new name can't take another's alias, and renaming back drops it (from 08).
- **note**: a post-it pinned to a point (`note_N`, from 08): `x, y, z` (y the surface it stands on), `text` (with `@skill` / `#tag` references), an optional `label` of up to 3 characters on its flag (a note with one is a **flag**, like `TK`; without, a round pin), a `color` (default yellow) and a `status`, `open` or `done`. Drawn as a pin at a constant size on screen, over everything; a done one is gray and faint, and hovering shows its first line. It moves, turns, mirrors and copies with its group like a line, and has no size, kind or tags. Open notes are work items: counted in `counts`, and listed in every outline's `notes` whatever the depth. The inspector has its text, flag and Done.
- **op**: the smallest reversible scene change (`add`, `remove`, `update`, and `order` for the outliner's reordering) inside a command.
- **palette**: the fixed set of color keys a box can use: the neutrals `white` (#ffffff), `almost-white` (#ededed, the default) and `gray` (#c4c4c4), plus pastels (`blue`, `yellow`, ...). The keys are the contract, and the pastel hex values are tuned freely (from 03).
- **player camera**: the project's walk settings in `player.json`: eye height (1.65 m), speed (4 m/s), each preset's horizontal field of view (90° first, 60° third) and the third-person boom (3 m behind the eye, 0.4 m above, 0.5 m to the right) and whether the human shows. Edited in the **pause menu**, saved as it changes (not undoable), and shared with the agent's eye and walk renders (from 09).
- **room**: a hollow closed shape: a floor slab and walls, no ceiling. Default height 3 m. Its footprint is its outside, and its walls grow inward from it (see **wall thickness**).
- **rotation**: a box's turn around the vertical axis through its center, in degrees, counterclockwise seen from above. 0 = grid-aligned (from 03).
- **open scene**: the one scene the server has open, shared by every tab and the agent, or none. Undo acts within it (from 04).
- **project**: a folder of scenes for one game, with a name and a description the agent reads. Its ID is a slug of its name at creation, and it holds `scenes/` plus the empty semantic folders `abilities/`, `entities/` and `rules/` (from 04).
- **reference**: `@name` (a skill) or `#name` (a tag) in a description or a note: a sigil at the start or after a non-word character, then a name starting with a letter (so emails, `room #2` and markdown headings aren't references). Known ones are tinted in the editor (skills amber, tags green), unknown ones underlined and listed; the agent gets a warning for one it writes (`findRefs` in `library.ts`, from 08).
- **review (topic)**: `get_guide review`, how the agent checks its work as a level designer: shots as requirements, reveals (the view from each area's entrance), wayfinding (walking the critical path: something to steer by in every frame), landmarks, metrics measured from the shapes, structure (a sheet), and **blockout discipline**: detail where the player's attention is, decoration as one volume, repeats as entities (from 09).
- **requirement (captioned shot)**: a shot with a caption is a claim about the level from its camera ("the flag reads from the approach"). After a change that may touch its view, the agent re-checks it (`render_view { view: "shots" }`) and says whether the claim still holds, or what it traded. An uncaptioned shot is just a picture (from 09).
- **scene**: the full design state the server owns: the `view`, the `selection` and all nodes (`scene.nodes`, from 03). The editor and the agent both read and edit the same scene. From 04 it's one scene (one level) of a project, saved on disk in `scenes/<id>/scene.json` after every step, and its ID is a slug of its name at creation.
- **selection (scene)**: the IDs of the selected nodes, reported in `scene.selection` so the agent knows what "this" means. Last tab wins, not undoable (from 03).
- **shape**: any node that isn't a group: a box, a cylinder, a free-form, a line or a ramp (from 07). The first three are **closed shapes** (a footprint, a kind, an elevation and a height; `ClosedShape` / `isClosed` in the code); a line has none of those. Closed shapes and ramps are **solids** (`Solid` / `isSolid`: a kind and a mesh, so holes cut them; only closed shapes can be holes). Each type has its own ID counter (`box_N`, `cylinder_N`, `freeform_N`, `line_N`). The agent draws them with `draw_shapes`, each with a `type` (from 06).
- **shot** (`shot_N`): a **capture** kept with the camera that took it, in the document it was taken in (a scene, or an entity in Edit entity mode), in `shots/` next to its `scene.json`: `shot_N.png` and its record in `shots.json` (caption, `createdBy`, `createdAt`, size, camera, and `seq`, the document's history step then, so what changed since can be told). IDs per document, never reused. Not a scene edit: no undo, and deleting one is permanent. The server reads the size from the PNG and serves it at `/shots/<project>/<scenes|entities>/<id>/<shot>.png` (from 09).
- **seed (array)**: the integer an array's noise, entity choice and scatter come from (from 10): the same seed, the same look on every load. Each item's randomness comes from a hash of the seed and its index, so adding items leaves the others as they were; a scatter draws from one stream of the seed, so a larger count keeps the earlier items. **Reroll** (the dice by the inspector's seed field, from 10.2) picks a new seed, one step (`Reroll array_4`).
- **sight check (`check_sight`)**: the agent's text answer to "is it in view?" (from 13): from eyes (a point, a node to stand on, or the walking human) to targets, the share of about 15 points over each target that rays reach, and every blocker along the way, nearest first (items as `array_1/12`). Rays pass through holes that cut what they hit; hidden nodes and `ignore` don't block. Renders are for how it looks.
- **skill**: something the player can do (`@telekinesis`), in the project library: a name, a description, and optionally the tags it acts on (telekinesis acts on `#light`). The agent designs around the skills (from 08).
- **skip (array)**: the item indices an array leaves out (from 10), e.g. the merlons over a gate. Indices follow the layout's order, so changing the count or the path can move which item an index is.
- **snap**: rounding a drawn footprint to the nearest 0.5 m. On by default, and holding `Cmd/Ctrl` while drawing turns it off (from 03; `Alt` in 02).
- **snap (vertical)**: rounding heights to the nearest 0.05 m. It's also the minimum height (from 02).
- **standing on (`on`)**: an instance or array placed on another node's walking surface, kept up to date (from 13): an instance's y is the top under its pivot, an array's items each drop onto what's under them, and an array **on an array** stands its item i on that array's item i (its place, turn and top), with no layout of its own. Giving it a height, or moving it up or down without what it stands on, unlinks it; the inspector's **Linked** section has **Unlink**.
- **tag**: a property of things (`#climbable`), in the project library: a name and an optional description. Groups, closed shapes and ramps carry `tags` (not lines), stored by the tag's name at the time; a tag must exist to be given. A deleted tag drops out of the outline and the counts but stays stored, so undo restores it (from 08).
- **taper**: a volume's `taper`, a fraction from 0 (straight sides, left out of the data) to 1 (the top is a point): the top is the outline scaled by 1 − taper toward its center, so a box at 1 is a pyramid and a cylinder a cone. A free-form scales toward its outline's centroid. Set with the inspector's taper slider, which previews while dragged. Making a volume a room drops its taper and bevel (from 07).
- **through line**: a line whose points come from **stops**, kept up to date (from 13): node IDs, array items (`array_3/5`), every item (`array_3/*`) or a range (`array_3/2..6`), each the center of its **walking surface**, joined by jump arcs (`style: "jumps"`, peaking `apex` above the higher stop) or straight. A removed stop drops out; editing its points, or moving it without its stops, unlinks it.
- **uses**: how many nodes carry a tag or name a skill or tag in a group's description, and in how many of the project's scenes; the Library panel and `get_library` show them (from 08).
- **view**: what the editor currently shows: the focus point, the yaw and `bounds`, the axis-aligned box around the visible ground. The editor reports it to the server so the agent knows where to draw. From 07B it also carries `isolated`, the node the human has isolated, if any.
- **view bar**: the small bar at the top right (from 07B), next to the error panel: the **holes** toggle (off hides the hole ghosts, so only what they cut away shows, and hidden holes can't be clicked unless selected; it was the status bar's **Show holes** in 07), the **grid** toggle, the **notes** toggle, and while a node is isolated its name with ✕. For clean views and images; for this tab only. From 14 there's a **lines** toggle and a **cursor** toggle (the 3D cursor), and the toggles are remembered per viewer. From 09 it ends with the **shutter** (a camera) and the **Shots panel**'s button with the count of shots. From 12 it ends with the **theme toggle**.
- **volume**: a solid box, something you stand on or bump into. Default height 0.25 m.
- **walking surface**: where a player stands on a node (from 13, `src/shared/surfaces.ts`): a volume's top, a room's floor or wall top, a ramp's surface, an instance's or item's highest level face under its pivot. Item lines' `top`, standing on and through lines use it.
- **wall thickness (`wall`)**: a room's walls' thickness in meters, 0.2 by default (left out of the data then) and at least 0.05, grown **inward** from the footprint, so the footprint is the room's outside. Two rooms that touch have two walls back to back; overlap them by a wall's thickness to share one. A room too narrow for an inside is drawn solid. Set in the inspector's wall thickness field (for selected rooms, and the next room in the drawing tools). A room that becomes a volume drops it (from 07; before, every wall was 0.2 m, centered on the footprint edge).
- **world unit (u)**: the coordinate unit of the scene. It's abstract for now and becomes meters in 02 (1 u = 1 m). At default zoom 1 u = 20 px. The data never stores pixels.
- **scale (an instance's or array's)**: a uniform factor on its entity's shapes around its pivot (from 14), 1 by default and then not stored, 0.01–100. Everything in meters grows with it (walls, heights), as with scaling any shape.
- **transform (`transform_nodes`)**: one step that optionally copies nodes, then scales, turns and mirrors them about one pivot (the bottom center of their bounds by default), then moves the pivot to `to` (from 14). Scaling grows every length, walls, heights and steps included; what isn't in meters keeps (angles, taper and bevel, a line's thickness). The way to make the human's sketch again at scale.

## System

- **find_nodes**: the agent's lookup: the nodes matching every filter given (part of a name, a type, a kind, inside a group, near a point), one compact line each with the path of group names and bounds, at most 100. The way to resolve a name to an ID without reading the scene (from 08).
- **guide (`get_guide`)**: the detail of the server instructions, one topic at a time (shapes, volumes, holes, ramps, lines, groups), which the agent reads before using a type for the first time in a session. The instructions keep a short core, and errors and warnings name the topic to read (from 08).
- **MCP server / tools**: the `/mcp` endpoint Claude Code connects to. Current tools: `get_scene`, `find_nodes`, `get_guide`, `get_library` and `update_library` (from 08), `draw_shapes` (was `draw_boxes` until 06), `update_nodes`, `remove_nodes`, `move_nodes` (with `copy`, from 05), `rotate_nodes`, `mirror_nodes` (from 05), `transform_nodes` (from 14), `group_nodes`, `ungroup`.
- **outline**: what `get_scene` returns by default (from 08): the children of a `root` group (the top level by default) down to `depth` levels, each group with its description, bounds and `contains`, and `collapsed: true` when its contents aren't listed; shapes in full. The selected nodes always come too (`selected`), `full: true` lists everything, and a scene of 40 nodes or fewer comes back in full. On a 290-node castle it's about 3% of the old full output.
- **render_view**: the agent's look at its work (from 09): the server asks the editor tab on screen that had focus last to render, and the image comes back with a text saying what it shows. Views: **sheet** (the default: a labeled top-down plan plus views from the northeast, south and west, in one image), **plan** (top-down, north up, with a grid and a scale bar), **node** (a close-up of `ids`), **eye** (what a player sees from a point, or from where the human walks), **walk** (a **walk strip**: numbered frames at eye height along a line or ramp) **shot** (a stored shot's camera again, now), **shots** (the human's captioned shots, each as taken beside the same camera now: a **requirement** check) and **entities** (a **model sheet**: the library's entities from one angle, with the human for scale). **Labels** are letters (A, B, ..., AA), with the legend in the text. Hidden nodes and notes are left out. Without an editor connected, or with no answer in 20 s, it fails saying so (`render.ts`, `renderView.ts`). **get_shots** lists the human's shots, or gives one's image as taken.
- **server instructions**: the conventions text (units, boxes, groups, IDs, the view, copying) the MCP server sends once when a client connects, instead of repeating it in every tool description (from 05). From 08 it's a short core (about 3.6 kB, from 10.3 kB), with the detail in the **guide**.
- **server**: the single local process (`npm run dev`) that owns the open scene, saves it in the data folder, and serves the editor, the WebSocket (`/ws`) and MCP (`/mcp`).
- **dry run (`dry_run`)**: on the agent's edit tools (from 14): the whole call is checked and built, and the result says what it would do, with every error, but nothing changes (no step, no IDs used, nothing saved). Before a big batch, or to try a ramp's curve. A failing batch names each failing entry, by its `ref` too.
- **facing (an instance's)**: `rotation` given as where an instance's local +x faces instead of degrees (from 14): `{ toward: {x, z} or an ID }`, `{ away }` or `{ along: a line or ramp }` (the direction of travel where it passes nearest). Worked out once when drawn or changed, not kept (unlike **standing on**).
- **agent's scene**: the scene the human invited the agent to with **Work with agent** (from 14, `app.json`'s `agentScene`). While the human has it open the two share one store; when they open another scene of the project (or an entity), the server loads it into a store of its own (`agentDoc`), which the agent's tools act on and which saves itself every step. `get_scene` says so (`agent`), there's no human view, selection or walk there, and `render_view` sends its nodes with the job to a tab showing another scene. Ending it (✕, or another project) tells the agent once.
- **get_changes**: what changed in the agent's scene since a step, by default since the agent's own last step (from 14): the steps (number, who, what, when), the net diff by node (added as compact lines, removed, changed field by field: `height: 200 → 143`, `points: 3 → 4 (changed)`), and the human's edits to the library and entities meanwhile (`elsewhere`). `get_scene`'s `changes` says when the human has made steps since the agent's last. From a **change log** kept in memory per document (the last 500 steps, each with the nodes after it).
